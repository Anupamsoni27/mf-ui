📈 How to Track Stocks in Funds

1

Browse Stocks

Navigate to the Stocks page to see all available stocks. Use search and filters to find specific companies.

Tip: Sort by "Holding Count" to see which stocks are most popular among mutual funds.
2

View Stock Details

Click on any stock to see detailed information including:

  • Number of mutual funds holding this stock
  • Historical timeline of fund holdings
  • Recent additions and removals
  • Sector and company information
3

Analyze Fund Holdings

Go to the Funds page to see which mutual funds are holding specific stocks. Filter by date to see historical holdings.

Key Metric: "Holding Count" shows how many stocks a fund is currently holding.
4

Monitor Trends

Use the timeline charts to identify trends:

  • ↗ Upward trends indicate increasing institutional interest
  • ↘ Downward trends suggest funds are reducing positions
  • → Stable holdings show consistent fund confidence

🎓 Making Better Investment Decisions

Buy Signals

  • Increasing Fund Holdings: More mutual funds are adding the stock
  • High Holding Count: Many funds show confidence in the stock
  • Consistent Additions: Funds continuously increasing positions over time
  • Quality Funds Investing: Reputable funds are taking positions

Sell Signals

  • Decreasing Fund Holdings: Funds are reducing or exiting positions
  • Low Holding Count: Few funds show interest in the stock
  • Consistent Removals: Funds continuously selling over time
  • Major Fund Exits: Large, reputable funds are selling

📋 Best Practices

✓

Diversify Your Research

Don't rely solely on fund holdings. Combine this data with fundamental analysis, technical analysis, and market news.

✓

Look at Trends, Not Snapshots

A single data point doesn't tell the full story. Analyze trends over multiple quarters to understand the bigger picture.

✓

Consider Fund Quality

Not all mutual funds are equal. Pay attention to which specific funds are holding the stock, especially top-performing funds.

✓

Monitor Regularly

Fund holdings change quarterly. Check back regularly to stay updated on institutional investment patterns.
